X_eTaL Games

Small games written in X_eTaL, a typed array language: each one is written around one array idea and happens to be playable.

Horse race

Horse races were among the first games people wrote for computers: a row of horses, each moved forward by a random amount every round, until one crosses the finish line. They were natural programs for the time-sharing terminals of the 1970s, in BASIC and in APL, where the whole field fits in one vector. Here five horses run to 15, each moving 1, 2 or 3 a round; at the terminal you start with 10 coins, back a horse for a coin each race, and win 4 back if it wins.

a vector of racers moved by one expression; the leaders by a fork

Five horses race to 15, each running 1, 2 or 3 a round, all at once. The field is one vector of positions and a round is one expression; the track is a character matrix drawn by a table. Bet on a horse at the terminal.

Guess the number

Guess the number is one of the oldest computer games and often the first program a beginner writes: the computer thinks of a number from 1 to 100 and answers each guess with higher or lower. It is also the classic first lesson in searching: always guessing the middle of what is still possible (a binary search) finds any number in at most seven guesses. Type a number and press Enter.

every candidate against every guess by one table; all games played at once

The smallest COR24 BASIC game, at a terminal: higher or lower until you have it. Its scripted game checks every candidate from 1 to 100 against every guess at once, and plays all 100 games in lockstep.

Trek adventure

Star Trek: Decaying Orbit is a short text adventure in the style of the type-in listings that computer magazines printed for home computers: rooms, a few items and a clock, played by picking numbered commands. You wake alone on the bridge of the Enterprise with the ship falling out of orbit; find the toolkit and the engine coupler, prep the relay in engineering and install the coupler within 30 turns, while Klingon boarders roam the ship. Pick a command by number (8 is help); GO, TAKE and USE then ask for a room or an item.

text as a table of rows picked by masks; refusals as data

Star Trek: Decaying Orbit, a text adventure: restore the Enterprise's engines before its orbit decays, past a locked armory and Klingon boarders. No branching code for the story: every line is a row of a table with a key and a condition, a room is one mask, a turn updates the state vector.

Sudoku

a candidate tensor; every single at once by sums over units; rounds to a fixed point
